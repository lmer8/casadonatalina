Notebook #4

The reason why

20 August 2026

I know science from the inside, and I have cultivated writing throughout my career. At this point, these two strands of my experience come together naturally.

Some people have asked me: but why a website? What's the point?

I said that the point is to make the words of my parents available to a wider audience, publish their books, and create a virtual house that resembles the one they created during their lives.

It is true, but this is just the beginning – it's the part about my parents. Let's now talk about the "me" part. Why do I spend time building and maintaining a multilingual website about books, encounters around books, and so on?

Well, this has less to do with my parents and more to do with who I am.

I am not a journalist. Nor a translator. Nor an HTML programmer.

I am a person who, after starting a publishing house and a website to make my family's voices available, realized that these things are actually part of who I am.

I am an engineer who has spent a career working with complex scientific topics and communicating them to different audiences, but I have also cultivated my writing and communication skills.

What attracts me to writing, maintaining a website, publishing and translating… is the opportunity to make interesting ideas, difficult concepts, or surprising experiences meaningful to other people. And, of course, to do this in different languages. Because communication is (and always has been) also about languages.

So alongside my "real job", I have written and published books and articles, created and edited content for my own multilingual publishing project, and worked extensively across languages and audiences. I have also undertaken formal training in creative writing. These activities have given me a different perspective on language, narrative and the relationship between writer and reader.

The urge to publish my parents’ books gave me the opportunity to bring this combination of scientific expertise, curiosity and writing to Casa Donatalina — and to create a space where I can share ideas, stories and passions with others, and hopefully make them meaningful to someone else.

So, in the end, that's the point: to open the doors of my house and let you meet its books and passions.

Notebook #3

Why I translated my book into English

31 July 2026

When I published Sogni, comunisti e altri disastri, I did so with the hope of entertaining people, of telling a story that could bring a few hours of joy to those who read it.

But stories are meant to be shared, and I soon realized that many of the people I care about do not speak Italian and were therefore excluded from the stories of Casa Donatalina.

So it was for them that I decided to translate the book into English.

It sounds easy: you say, “I'll translate it into English, reach a wider audience, make my friends happy”...

But actually it is an extremely difficult undertaking.

As I translated, I soon realized just how many Italian cultural references there are in the book. And I had to start adding notes, explaining things, and trying to find a way to say, in credible English, this or that thing that seems obvious and trivial until you actually have to translate it.

Who is Alfredino? Who is Pertini? Why the escalator?

It was a difficult yet extremely educational journey. I learned that a translator does not simply translate words: they translate a whole world of cultural references, bringing a setting, a history and a culture into the words they choose.

So no, you can't simply have AI do it. It's not the same thing.

I'm not sure I did it well, but I did it with passion and with people I care about in mind. So, if it's not perfect, you know who to complain to!

If you have comments, criticism or suggestions for improving the translation, I will be happy to read them.

Notebook #2

A House Full of Books

30 July 2026

There are more than six thousand books in Casa Donatalina. Some were read once and then forgotten for years. Others have been reread, underlined, lent out, and found again. Some came from friends, while others have been part of our family for decades.

This website was also born from the desire not to let all this treasure remain on the shelves to just get dusty.

We can't put six thousand books online, of course. But we can tell the stories of the ones we loved, the ones that made us think, and the ones that kept us company at a particular moment in our lives.

And perhaps, through our reading, introduce some of them to you as well. In the Meetings section, as this website grows, you will find some of the books we have encountered along the way.

Notebook #1

Why Casa Donatalina

29 July 2026

Today, a small project that means a lot to me officially begins: Casa Donatalina. The name comes from my parents' names (Donata and Natalino), and casa means home.

It is a space dedicated to my family's books (but not only). Books that are different from one another, but connected by a common thread: the desire to preserve and share stories that deserve to keep living. The desire to share these books then gave rise to the idea of expanding Casa Donatalina to include other encounters, but you will discover that as you explore the website.

For now, I would like to start by introducing you to the first book.

It is the novel written by my father: Il Parco della Contessa. You can find it in the Books section of this website.

When my dad passed away, the manuscript was still unfinished. I decided to collect his notes, study his style and complete the work with one precise goal: to make sure that, as much as possible, it would be his book, not mine.

It was a long, emotional and, at times, difficult journey. But it was also one of the most beautiful ways I have found to continue a dialogue with him.

If you love stories born from passion, memory and the desire not to let words be lost, I hope this book will spark your curiosity.

In the next notebooks, I will tell you more about the novel, its characters and the journey that brought me here.
